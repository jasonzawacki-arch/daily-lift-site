Daily Lift privacy policy

Draft. This policy describes the Daily Lift beta and takes effect when the beta opens on Google Play. Last updated October 18, 2026.

Daily Lift (Android, Google Play id app.dailylift) is made by an independent developer. This policy explains what the app does with your information, including during the beta. Questions or requests: jz.dailyapp@gmail.com.

The short version

  • There is no account and no sign-in. You don't give me your name or email to use the app.
  • Everything you log is stored on your phone. I have no server that receives it, and I can't see it.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and no selling of data.
  • Information leaves your phone only when you send it: a backup file or export you save or share, a feedback email you choose to send, or (optional) a label photo or product name sent to an AI provider you pick, with your own key.
  • You can delete everything by uninstalling the app or clearing its storage.

What the app stores on your phone

Whatever you choose to log, for example:

  • workouts and programs: exercises, sets, weights, reps, reps in reserve, notes, and pain scores in injury mode;
  • your daily checklist: supplements, medications and other items, their schedules and the times you checked them off, doses, injection sites, vial and supply counts, and a dose-change log;
  • body and health entries: sleep, body weight, blood pressure, resting heart rate, body fat, measurements, progress photos, meals, protein, fiber, water, gut symptoms and lab results you type in;
  • settings, including an AI provider key if you add one;
  • a short list of recent app error messages (the error text and where in the app it happened, never the values you logged), kept so you can choose to include them in a feedback email.

This data stays in the app's private storage on your device, which Android keeps apart from other apps. Your phone's own encryption protects the stored data. The app turns off Android's automatic cloud backup, so your log is not copied to your Google device backup.

App lock (optional) asks for a PIN you set (or, where the app offers it, your phone's own screen lock) before the app opens. App lock keeps someone who picks up your phone out of the app. Without it, anyone who can unlock your phone can open the app.

Estimates the app shows (for example estimated medication levels, estimated one-rep maxes or insights) are calculated on your phone from your own entries. They are for tracking only, not medical advice.

When information leaves your phone

Only in these cases, each started by you:

Backups and exports. Backup files, the Transfer code, CSV exports, PDF summaries and workout share images are created on your phone. They go wherever you save or send them (your files, an email, a messaging app). I don't receive them. A backup file includes your progress photos unless you turn off the photos option when you save it, so treat the file as private.

Feedback emails. "Send feedback" opens your own email app with a message addressed to jz.dailyapp@gmail.com. You see the whole message and decide whether to send it. If you tick "Include diagnostics", it adds the app version and build, basic device details (the Android version and, where the phone reports it, the model), screen size, and the last few app error messages. It never adds your health or workout data. If you send it, I receive your email address and what you wrote. I use feedback only to reply and to fix problems. Bug reports are copied into my private task list without your name, email address or any health details. I delete feedback emails once the issue is answered and closed, and keep none longer than 12 months. The address is a Gmail account, so Google stores those emails under its own privacy policy.

AI label reading and price lookup (optional). If you add your own API key for Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI or Google (Gemini):

  • "Read a label" sends the photo you take or pick (shrunk to at most 1280 pixels) straight from your phone to the provider you chose. A pharmacy label can show your name and medication, so only photograph what you are comfortable sending.
  • "Prices" sends the product's brand, name and container size to that provider, which searches the web. Items marked as prescription never get a price lookup.

These requests go directly to the provider under your key and their privacy terms; they never pass through me. Your key is stored only on your phone and is never put in backups. Without a key, nothing is sent.

Copy for AI. This copies a text summary of a workout or recent weeks to your clipboard. The app sends nothing; you decide where to paste it.

Links you open. The Play Store link and retailer links from a price lookup open in the Play Store or your browser, under their own policies.

Web font. The first time the app starts, it downloads its typeface from Google Fonts. That request tells Google your device's IP address and browser details, as any web request does. No app data is included. Your phone normally keeps a copy afterwards.

Google Drive sync (not in the beta). A later version may offer optional sync through your own Google Drive. If you turn it on, the app keeps a copy of your log (not your photos) in a hidden app folder in your Drive that only Daily Lift can open, and stores your Google account email on your phone to show which account is linked. It goes to your Drive, not to me. This feature is turned off in the beta.

Inside the Claude app (development builds only). A private test version of Daily Lift runs inside the Claude app during development. There, it keeps an automatic backup copy (without photos) in your own private Claude storage, under Anthropic's terms. The Android app from Google Play never does this.

What I don't do

  • No advertising, and no ad or analytics libraries in the app.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no profiling.
  • No selling or renting of your data, and no sharing of it beyond what you send yourself (above).
  • No automatic crash reporting from the app itself.
  • No account and no server of mine that stores your data.

Google Play and Android

Google Play shows developers aggregated statistics, such as install counts, device types and countries, and, from people who allow it in their Android settings, crash and performance reports (Android vitals). Google collects these under its own privacy policy; they don't include what you log in the app. I use them only to find and fix problems.

Beta testers

During the closed beta on Google Play:

  • You join through a Google Group. As the group's owner, I can see members' email addresses; other members can't. I use the group only to run the beta: announcing new versions and asking for feedback. You can leave the group at any time.
  • The beta collects nothing automatically. It has no analytics and no hidden reporting. I learn what works and what doesn't only from feedback you send and from Google Play's statistics above.
  • All Pro features are unlocked during the beta. Nothing about your data changes when the app is released; the release arrives as a normal update.
  • Testers can also send private feedback through Google Play, which Google passes to me under its own terms.

Deleting your data

  • On your phone: delete single entries in the app, or delete everything by uninstalling Daily Lift or going to Android Settings > Apps > Daily Lift > Storage > Clear storage (called Clear data on some phones). This can't be undone, so save a backup first if you want to keep anything.
  • Backup files and exports you saved: delete them where you saved them.
  • AI provider requests: handled under the provider's own retention terms.
  • Google Drive sync (when available): turn it off in the app, then remove the hidden app data in Google Drive (Settings > Manage apps > Daily Lift > Delete hidden app data).
  • Feedback emails and the tester group: email jz.dailyapp@gmail.com to have your emails deleted, and leave the Google Group to remove your address from it.

Because there is no account, there is no account to delete.

Security

Your log never reaches a server of mine. Every connection the app makes (to an AI provider you chose, Google Fonts, and later Google Drive) uses HTTPS. Keys you add are kept on your phone only. Security problems in the app are fixed first. To report one, email jz.dailyapp@gmail.com.

Children

Daily Lift is meant for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and I don't knowingly receive information from them.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EU, UK or California), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal data held about you. The only personal data I hold is what you send me by email and, if you're a beta tester, your address in the tester group. To make a request, email jz.dailyapp@gmail.com.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with a new effective date, and the app will mention important changes in its update notes.
